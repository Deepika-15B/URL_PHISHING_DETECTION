Complexity and deployment analysis
------------------------------------
FNN
- Architecture: Dense(64)-Dense(32)-output
- Hidden/decision layers: 2
- Trainable parameters: 3457
- Computational complexity: Two compact dense layers; lowest O(sum layer products) cost.
- Ease of deployment: Straightforward Keras serving with compact dense operations.
DNN
- Architecture: Dense(128)-Dense(64)-Dense(32)-output
- Hidden/decision layers: 3
- Trainable parameters: 13953
- Computational complexity: Three dense hidden layers; moderate O(sum layer products) cost.
- Ease of deployment: Straightforward Keras serving with compact dense operations.
Wide & Deep
- Architecture: Wide linear branch + 3-layer dense branch
- Hidden/decision layers: 4
- Trainable parameters: 14393
- Computational complexity: Two parallel branches; highest dense-operation overhead of the Keras models.
- Ease of deployment: Straightforward Keras serving; extra dual-input wiring.
TabNet
- Architecture: Five-step attentive TabNet encoder with sparse feature masks
- Hidden/decision layers: 5
- Trainable parameters: 32888
- Computational complexity: Five sequential attentive decision steps; more control-flow and activation overhead than dense networks.
- Ease of deployment: Portable PyTorch artifact, but requires PyTorch TabNet runtime and has higher serving complexity.

Final recommendation
--------------------
- Highest test-set performance under F1/ROC-AUC/accuracy tie-breaking: DNN.
- Recommended Flask integration: FNN, provided its prediction metrics remain effectively tied with the alternatives shown above. It offers the simplest model, smallest dense architecture, and avoids TabNet's sequential-attention and runtime-dependency overhead.
- Trade-off: the table provides the measured speed and memory evidence. If FNN is within trivial metric differences of the leaders, those differences do not by themselves justify DNN, Wide & Deep, or TabNet complexity for real-time URL checks.
- Statistical interpretation: this benchmark measures deployment behavior, not a new statistical test. The previous corrected-split results should be considered practically tied when their errors differ on only a handful of samples; a paired test or external holdout is required to claim a meaningful quality advantage.
